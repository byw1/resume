Four parts, one record

The app is where you look. The conversation is where you work.

Everything below is reachable from a conversation — 64 tools over MCP, plus the screens for when you want to see, adjust or print the result.

Brain

Everything you know about every job you've had. No length limit, no structure required — numbers, stories, praise, screw-ups. The raw material everything else is built from.

Resumes

Tailored documents assembled from that material. Harvard format by default, live preview, real server-rendered PDF, and a public link for application forms.

Pipeline

Applications on a board, list or calendar, with follow-ups that chase you. The dashboard reads your funnel and tells you which step is losing people.

CRM

Companies and the people at them, as records of their own. Research outlives the application it started from.

The rule the whole thing is built on

Nothing on a resume is invented. Every connected assistant is instructed to never make up experience, employers, dates or metrics. Every bullet traces back to something you put in your brain — and when a posting asks for evidence you don't have, it says so instead of writing it anyway. The quiet upgrade — a credit that becomes a hire, a "helped with" that becomes a "led" — is the way resumes actually go wrong, and it is the specific thing this refuses to do.
